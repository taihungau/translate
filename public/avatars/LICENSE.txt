The talking faces on the course (woman.glb, man.glb)

Both heads are the "Head 2.0" sample busts that itSeez3D publishes on https://avatarsdk.com/avatars/
to show what Avatar SDK makes from one photo. They are samples of a commercial product: no open
licence comes with them. The woman's long hair is the hair of the "avaturn.glb" example avatar of
the TalkingHead project (https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead), created at Avaturn
(https://avaturn.me) for non-commercial use.

They are used here only in this personal, non-commercial English course. For any other use,
replace them with avatars you have the rights to: Avatar SDK and Avaturn both make one from a
photo, and TalkingHead's README (Appendix A) lists free ways, including the CC0 MPFB / MakeHuman
route. The engine (public/avatar.js) needs a head with ARKit face shapes, the 14 Oculus visemes
(viseme_aa …) and the bones chest, neck, head, eyeLeft and eyeRight.

What was changed, for phones (2.3 and 1.7 MB instead of 27 MB each):
- smooth normals added (the samples have none); skin, eyes, teeth and the inside of the mouth
  split into parts so that each can be shaded in its own way;
- the 25 face shapes the course never uses removed, the rest stored as 16-bit numbers; everything
  compressed with meshopt;
- woman.glb: the hair set on the head (moved so that the two models' eyes meet, bound to the
  bust's bones, its texture saved as WebP with the strands' colour spread under their edges); the
  head's own painted hair is pushed in by up to 7 mm and darkened under it.

The lips are timed with TalkingHead's English lip-sync module (MIT License, Copyright (c) 2023-2024
Mika Suominen) and everything is drawn with three.js (MIT License, Copyright © 2010-2026 three.js
authors), both loaded from cdn.jsdelivr.net.
